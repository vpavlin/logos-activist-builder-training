# λ | Building on the Ecosystem

**Session 2 — Eco Dev — 30 minutes**

Short on purpose. The rest of the time is yours.

---

## Who is an activist builder?

Someone who understands the technology and takes part in Logos Circles.

Standing in both places is the point. You need to know the Logos stack well enough
to map local-first, peer-to-peer architecture onto a real problem — and to be
close enough to the problem to know which one matters.

---

## Support the Circles

Every Circle should be able to count on a builder when technology, research or
design can move an issue forward. Join one in your city, dial into their meetings,
or take a problem away and work on it asynchronously.

Start from what the Circle actually needs, not from what the technology can do.

---

## Spot what repeats

The most valuable work happens between Circles.

Support enough communities and the same problems keep appearing: crowdfunding,
organising people, collecting testimony. Tools, workflows and research that one
Circle needed and thirty could use.

Turning those into something reusable is **parallel infrastructure**. Nobody owns
that job by default — every campaign is under pressure to solve today's problem
and move on.

> **On the name.** Václav Benda's *parallel polis*, 1978: build your own
> structures rather than only confronting the official ones. Charter 77 did
> exactly that, and Prague has a Paralelní Polis named after the essay.

---

## Build with other builders

If there is enough activity in your city, organise a **Builders Meetup**.

Not another tech meetup. A practical space to bring problems from Circles, share
what you have worked out, and hack on things together.

Builders who exchange tools compound. Builders who work alone each solve the same
problem separately.

---

## Who you work with

Eco Dev is your first contact. Immediate problems they help with. Deeper ones — a
missing feature, a limit in the protocol — they route to the team that owns that
piece. Working out who maintains what is not your job.

- **Discord** — day to day
- **GitHub issues** — anything that should outlive a conversation
- **Dev Clubs** — meetings and live streams; watch before you commit

Everything in the open. Ship fast, talk early. "It broke at step four" is a
contribution, not an admission.

---

## Defining the work

Lambda Prizes and RFPs fund work that is too big for volunteer time.

The first ask is not that you apply. It is that you help write them — you are
closer to the problem than anyone scoping it from a desk.

---

## Over to you

- What is your Circle working on right now?
- Which part would another Circle have to build all over again?
- What would it take to make that part reusable?
