## Who this is for

Activists and organisers. Some will have written code, most won't. The goal is **not** to
make anyone a Logos developer in 60 minutes. The goal is:

1. They leave knowing **what the stack can do** and roughly **how it's shaped**.
2. They leave knowing **where to go next** — to build themselves, or to find people who will.
3. They leave with an accurate sense of **what's ready and what isn't**, so nobody bounces
   off a broken quickstart and concludes the whole thing is vapour.

## How the material is written

Each session is written as **prose you can read aloud or turn into e-learning text**, not as
bullet-point slides. Sections are self-contained so they can be lifted into modules later.

Three recurring boxes carry the load:

- **What this means for organising** — the activist-facing "so what". Never skip these; they
  are the reason the room is in the room.
- **Honest status** — what actually works today vs. what is a draft or a plan. This is a
  credibility feature, not a disclaimer. Say it out loud.
- **Where to go next** — the pointer. Every concept ends with somewhere to click.

## Timing discipline

30 minutes is roughly **3,500–4,000 spoken words**. Both sessions are written a little
richer than that on purpose, so there's material to cut live and material to keep for the
e-learning version. Each session has a run-of-show table at the top with a suggested cut
order — the sections marked *(cut first)* are the ones to drop when you're running long.
